# Compliance Guide — Hours of Service Rules (Property-Carrying)
codes:
system: compliance

A compact reference to the clocks that telematics tracks for every driver.

## The clocks
- **11-hour driving limit**: no more than 11 hours of driving after 10 consecutive hours
  off duty.
- **14-hour duty window**: driving is not permitted beyond the 14th consecutive hour after
  coming on duty, regardless of breaks.
- **30-minute break**: required after 8 cumulative hours of driving without at least a
  30-minute interruption (off duty, sleeper, or on-duty not driving).
- **60/70-hour cycle**: no driving after 60 hours on duty in 7 days or 70 hours in 8 days;
  a 34-hour restart resets the cycle.

## What a violation risk looks like
The driver is still driving, the remaining drive minutes are falling, and the estimated
time to destination exceeds what is left. A margin (remaining minus estimated) below
15 minutes is a near miss; a negative margin means the driver cannot legally finish.

## Dispatcher playbook
1. Message the driver early (at least 60 minutes before the clock runs out) with the
   remaining time and a safe place to stop. Never instruct a driver to keep driving.
2. Look for a rested spare driver or an idle unit near the route to hand off the load.
3. Reassign the trip when the deficit is more than about 15 minutes; otherwise coordinate a
   stop and a later ETA with the customer.
4. Log the decision. Repeated near misses on the same lane indicate a planning problem,
   not a driver problem.

## Near misses are not violations
Arriving with 5–15 minutes left is tight but legal. Managers may choose not to be alerted
for a driver who consistently manages the clock; the system should learn that preference.
